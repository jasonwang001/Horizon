### [Microsoft-backed letter and AI employees urge nuanced open-weight policy](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/2/open-letters/#atom-everything) ⭐️ 7.0/10

On July 24, 2026, a Microsoft-shepherded open letter titled &quot;Open Weights and American AI Leadership&quot; was published, signed by 235 AI-adjacent companies including NVIDIA, Amazon, Y Combinator, The Linux Foundation, and later OpenAI, arguing against any US government ban or restrictions on open-weight AI models. The letter directly counters &quot;safety&quot; concerns that have circulated in Washington, notably after the reported suspension of Claude Fable 5, and explicitly endorses distillation as a legitimate model-improvement technique rather than misappropriation. Anthropic did not sign; three days later it issued its own position stating it has never advocated a ban on open-weights models, while CEO Dario Amodei warned about authoritarian governments building powerful AI and called for a crackdown on industrial-scale distillation operations. On July 28, a separate open letter, &quot;Pacing the Frontier,&quot; appeared with signatures from 1,324 employees of frontier AI companies, including Jakub Pachocki, Ilya Sutskever, Dario Amodei, and Jack Clark, requesting an international effort to deliberately pace the frontier of automated AI development. These letters represent a high-profile, partially conflicting industry push to shape US AI policy on open weights, distillation, and accelerated AI research.

**「Background」** Open-weight AI models make their trained parameters publicly available, allowing researchers and developers to inspect, run, and fine-tune them, unlike fully closed models. In mid-2026, the US government signaled possible restrictions on open-weight models due to safety worries, and had reportedly suspended access to Claude Fable 5, creating an urgent policy debate among AI companies. The two open letters reflect that debate: one argues open weights improve security and competition, while the other, driven by concerns about automated AI research and competitive pressure, calls for deliberate pacing of frontier AI development.

**「Impact」** The letters give US policymakers a visible split among leading AI companies: 235 companies including Microsoft, NVIDIA, Amazon, and OpenAI oppose open-weight restrictions, while Anthropic and thousands of frontier-lab employees emphasize safety, governance, and the risks of uncontrolled AI acceleration. This may influence forthcoming US regulatory decisions on open-weight models, distillation, and international AI coordination.
