### [NAT and the Original Sin of Internet Centralization](https://dreamstation.systems/personal/ntppost.html) ⭐️ 7.0/10

An essay on dreamstation.systems argues that Network Address Translation \(NAT\) is one of the earliest causes of internet centralization, because it removed the public endpoint from most devices and made running a server no longer trivial. The piece contends that NAT trained users to accept a client-server model where devices talk to the cloud rather than to each other, a mindset originally born from IPv4 address scarcity. Community discussion includes Rusty Russell, the Linux NAT implementer, who apologizes for the design choices that prioritized squeezing more connections into one IP address at the cost of inbound routability and the erosion of self-hosting. Other commenters push back, arguing that ordinary NAT is acceptable when controllable and that it has protected insecure devices, while singling out Carrier Grade NAT as the genuinely harmful variant. The essay is a historical and architectural reflection rather than a report of a new development.

hackernews · robinpie · Aug 31, 02:23 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49504905)

**「Background: NAT and IP address scarcity」** Network address translation \(NAT\) was first formally proposed in RFC 1631 in 1994 to address IP address depletion and scaling in routing. By letting many devices share a single public IPv4 address, NAT relieved the immediate address shortage but also made those devices less accessible from the outside, a trade-off that the essay argues helped shift the internet toward a centralized, client-server model.

**「Community discussion」** Commenters disagree on whether NAT deserves the label &\#x27;original sin&\#x27;: Rusty Russell, the Linux NAT implementer, apologizes for his role in favoring connection density over public endpoints, while another commenter argues that regular NAT is fine when controllable and that Carrier Grade NAT is the truly harmful concept. A third commenter attributes the internet&\#x27;s security problems to designers mistakenly applying real-world norms to cyberspace.

### [Sliding-window attention beats linear attention on long-context reasoning](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1w3j1vw/slidingwindow_attention_beats_linear_on/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A new arXiv preprint \(2608.28444\) by Alexia Jolicoeur-Martineau, Rhea Sanjay Sukthanker, Pashmina Cameron, and Emy Gervais claims that sliding-window attention with sinks \(SWA\) achieves 2 to 10 times higher performance than linear-attention variants on long-context reasoning benchmarks, specifically Needle-in-a-Haystack and BABILong. The authors argue that the post-training-to-linear research line has not been properly compared to simpler baselines, and they strongly recommend switching to SWA instead of post-training linear models. They concede that linear attention may show some promise but likely requires training from scratch or extensive post-training to match SWA. The preprint is not yet peer-reviewed, and the full evidence is not available in the source item.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/Justgototheeffinmoon · Aug 31, 16:35

**「Background」** Standard transformer attention has quadratic cost in sequence length, motivating alternatives such as linear attention and sliding-window attention \(SWA\). Sliding-window attention restricts each token&\#x27;s attention to a local window, and adding attention sinks can help maintain performance on long sequences. The arXiv preprint 2608.28444 compares SWA with sinks to post-trained linear-attention variants on long-context reasoning tasks, reporting that SWA achieves 2 to 10 times higher performance on benchmarks like Needle-in-a-Haystack and BABILong.

**「Impact」** AI/ML teams investing post-training compute in linear-attention conversions for inference memory savings may be wasting effort: on the long-context reasoning tasks examined in the preprint \(Needle-in-a-Haystack and BABILong\), plain sliding-window attention with sinks achieved 2 to 10 times higher performance with no post-training. Before pursuing linear-attention pipelines, these teams should benchmark this simple baseline first, since it is extremely fast, low-memory, and cheap.

### [Temporal leakage in GNNs exposed by SynthFin-AML benchmark](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1w3imxy/your_gnn_is_probably_just_an_overcomplicated_mlp/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A Reddit post argues that standard GNN evaluations on dynamic transaction graphs are often invalid because message-passing over static snapshots or transductive random splits lets models see future edges during training, creating temporal leakage. To address this, the authors released SynthFin-AML v10.0, a synthetic dataset with 100k nodes and 1.2M edges, and introduced a strict 3-snapshot point-in-time split: train on edges up to Day 7, validate on edges up to Day 8, and test on edges up to Day 10. They also removed distribution leakage by making fraud and retail transaction amounts share the same lognormal distribution \(μ=8.517, σ=0.8\). Benchmarking a tuned LightGBM with 11 point-in-time graph features against inductive GraphSAGE on this strict temporal split yielded PR-AUC scores of 0.848 and 0.881 respectively, showing only a modest real gain for the GNN. The benchmark has been submitted upstream to PyTorch Geometric as PR \#10774.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/Glabmayt2075 · Aug 31, 16:21

**「Background」** GNNs compute node embeddings by aggregating information from neighboring nodes, so on a dynamic graph a model trained on a static snapshot can inadvertently use edges from later time steps. For example, if Node A sends funds to B on Day 2 and B sends to C on Day 10, a standard 2-hop GNN can pull the Day 10 edge into the loss calculation for Day 2, effectively looking into the future. Synthetic AML datasets often also allow models to cheat by relying on statistically separable transaction amounts, which SynthFin-AML aims to prevent by matching the amount distributions across fraud and retail transactions.

**「Impact」** Researchers and practitioners evaluating GNNs on dynamic financial graphs should adopt point-in-time splits and point-in-time feature engineering to avoid inflated results, since the source findings show that without such care a GNN can appear to perform far better than it truly does. The benchmark also suggests that GNNs may offer only a modest edge over well-tuned tree models on tabular financial data unless edge features are very dense.
