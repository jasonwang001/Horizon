### [US Government Backs OpenAI, Says AI Training Is Fair Use](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/us-government-backs-openai-new-york-times-copyright-case-2026-09-02/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The U.S. government filed an amicus brief in Manhattan federal court supporting OpenAI in its copyright dispute with The New York Times, arguing that training large language models on copyrighted works generally qualifies as fair use. The brief marks the U.S. government&\#x27;s first formal position on AI training copyright cases, although it is not legally binding. The Times criticized the government for siding with “trillion-dollar AI companies” at the expense of creators. The newspaper sued OpenAI and Microsoft in 2023, alleging unauthorized use of millions of its articles to train ChatGPT.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 3, 05:45

**「Background」** This dispute stems from The New York Times&\#x27; 2023 lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft, which alleged that the companies used millions of the newspaper&\#x27;s articles without permission to train ChatGPT. The core legal question is whether such training constitutes copyright infringement or is protected as &quot;fair use,&quot; a doctrine that permits limited unauthorized use of copyrighted material for purposes like commentary or transformation. In this context, the U.S. government&\#x27;s amicus brief, filed on September 2, 2026, argues that AI training should generally qualify as fair use because it is &quot;highly transformative,&quot; marking the government&\#x27;s first formal position on AI training and copyright.

**「Impact」** The non-binding U.S. government brief gives OpenAI and Microsoft stronger political and rhetorical support in their pending Manhattan fair-use defense, and it signals to AI developers and researchers that the executive branch currently views training on copyrighted works as broadly permissible. Publishers such as The New York Times still face an unresolved court decision, but the government&\#x27;s emphasis on transformative use and U.S. AI leadership may influence how courts and future rulemaking treat copyright liability.

### [Porting a 1993 Amiga game to Godot with an LLM reading 68000 assembly](https://babyloniantwins.com/blog/porting-a-1993-amiga-game-to-godot/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The author ported their 1993 Amiga game, originally written in Baghdad in MC68000 assembly, to Godot over roughly an evening using Claude to read and translate the assembly, with extra weekends and evenings spent preserving the game feel and preparing the release. Claude assembled the code with vasm on the author&\#x27;s Mac and iterated until the output was byte-identical to the original binaries, except for a mismatch of about 108 bytes. The discrepancy came from the original shipped files being saved as an in-memory snapshot of the already-running game from AsmOne, not clean assembler output. The author is releasing the original game for free and documented the process using decades of personal notes, git repositories, and analysis of how Claude worked.

hackernews · rabahs · Sep 3, 14:28 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49550375)

**「Background」** The game was built in 1993 in MC68000 assembly using AsmOne, which assembled directly into memory; the files on the original disks were therefore memory snapshots of a game that had already been running, rather than ordinary assembler output. Modern LLMs can assist reverse engineering and porting by translating low-level assembly into higher-level languages, and requiring the generated assembly to be byte-identical to the original provides a strong correctness check before moving the logic into a modern engine like Godot.

**「Impact」** For retro game developers and LLM-assisted porting practitioners, this account demonstrates a viable workflow in which byte-level assembly verification precedes translation, enabling a 68000-to-Godot port in about one evening plus iterative work on game feel.

**「Community discussion」** Commenters were enthusiastic, sharing similar successes such as converting a ZX81 memory dump into Go and asking for an engineering guide, debugging stories, and clarification on whether the game was inspired by Gods: Into the Wonderful. Others noted an interest in applying the same approach to forgotten games they did not author.

hackernews · vignesh\_warar · Sep 3, 19:45 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49555691)

The ARC-AGI-3 leaderboard lists verified results for OpenAI&\#x27;s GPT-6 Astra across many harness configurations, while prior verified scores include roughly 30.2% for Anthropic Claude Opus 5 and 7.78% for OpenAI GPT-5.6 Sol. Others questioned the benchmark&\#x27;s validity, asking whether minimizing moves in a snake-like puzzle game is a meaningful test of intelligence, and expressed concern that OpenAI might have had prior access or built a custom harness for ARC-AGI-3.
