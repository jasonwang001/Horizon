**「Background」** Asahi Linux is an open-source project that ports Linux to Apple Silicon Macs by reverse-engineering undocumented hardware and writing drivers that Apple does not provide. ### [DNS Abuse: New gTLD Registrations Fuel Scams](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/6/the-purpose-of-dns-is-to-spread-scams/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Simon Willison highlights a blog post by Terence Eden that points to an Interisle report indicating roughly 85 million new generic top-level domain \(gTLD\) registrations were made in 2025, with 8.5 million of those domains added to blocklists by May 2025. The report estimates that a 10% abuse rate is the likely floor, and the actual rate may be closer to 20%, meaning a large share of newly registered gTLDs are used for scams. The post frames DNS as a vector for criminal activity and notes that ICANN has reportedly been discussing this problem for years without sufficiently addressing it. The statistics specifically concern new gTLD registrations and suggest that domain-based scams are a persistent and widespread infrastructure issue.

rss · Simon Willison · Sep 6, 14:40

**「Background」** The Domain Name System \(DNS\) translates human-readable domain names into IP addresses, making it a foundational part of internet navigation. Generic top-level domains \(gTLDs\) include familiar suffixes like .com plus many newer suffixes introduced to expand available naming space, and ICANN oversees the domain name system&\#x27;s coordination. The concern is that domain registrars and new gTLD operators have created an environment where criminals can cheaply register thousands of domains for phishing, fraud, and other scams before they are detected.

**「Impact」** Users and security teams should expect that new gTLD domain registrations carry a materially high risk of being tied to scams, so caution, reputation checks, and blocklist monitoring are necessary when evaluating recently registered domains. The reported figures also pressure ICANN and registrars to tighten registration and abuse-handling practices, though concrete policy changes are not yet presented in this item.
