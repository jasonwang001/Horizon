The site describes the method as novel and reportsably enables extraction of hidden chain-of-thought outputs, raising questions about the effectiveness of encryption and jailbreaking barriers in commercial models. The discussion frames the practice in contrasting terms: some call it merely accessing outputs users already paid for, while others see it as undermining provider protections. The episode underscores growing tensions between transparency advocates and API providers.

hackernews · quantumgarbage · Aug 11, 13:22 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49257876)

**「Background」** Proprietary large language model APIs often hide their internal chain-of-thought reasoning traces from users, returning them as encrypted text that the client must send back with subsequent requests. Researchers demonstrated that these encrypted traces can be intercepted and replayed into weaker sibling models, which can be jailbroken to output the original reasoning in plaintext. This work exposes an architectural vulnerability affecting major LLM providers and has sparked debate over whether extracting such traces constitutes theft or acceptable use of model outputs.

**「Impact」** API users have already demonstrated that developer-prompt injection can force models like Codex to output encrypted compaction data as plaintext, proving that existing attempts to hide reasoning traces are bypassable. This practical workaround could undermine providers&\#x27; ability to monetize hidden reasoning and likely pressures them to adopt stronger countermeasures.

**「Community Discussion」** Commenters are divided: some argue that &\#x27;stealing&\#x27; is the wrong framing because users already paid for tokens and training on model outputs should be normal, while others share practical tricks for exposing hidden reasoning, such as injecting short developer prompts or using a &\#x27;deep\_think&\#x27; tool to surface internal chain-of-thought. There is also curiosity about whether the leakage was intentionally allowed and speculation that future models will refuse to disclose their reasoning.

### [HyperSAE applies Poincaré geometry to sparse autoencoders, cutting dead latents and MSE](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1vlpyh2/hypersae_decoupled_poincar%C3%A9_geometry_for_sparse/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

HyperSAE is a new PyTorch library that applies Poincaré hyperbolic geometry to Sparse Autoencoders \(SAEs\) for mechanistic interpretability, and the author reports concrete gains on Gemma-2-2B Layer 13 trained on 20M tokens of FineWeb-Edu using an NVIDIA L4. The architecture uses a decoupled dual-speed design: the forward pass and causal steering remain entirely Euclidean with zero inference overhead, while during training dictionary weights are projected into the Poincaré ball and an entailment cone loss organizes parent concepts near the origin and child concepts near the boundary. On the reported benchmarks, HyperSAE reduces reconstruction MSE by 9.8% \(4.5724 to 4.1232\), improves CE loss recovery from 75.5% to 78.9%, and cuts dead latents from 3.8% to 0.2%, with a small MMLU-Pro accuracy gain of 0.15 percentage points. The library includes co-activation queue tracking, a TriPartite loss combining reconstruction, L1 sparsity, and entailment, and a single-class trainer interface; it is installable via pip. These results are the author&\#x27;s own claims and have not been independently validated.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/visha1v · Aug 11, 18:37 · [Discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1vlpyh2/hypersae_decoupled_poincar%C3%A9_geometry_for_sparse/)

**「Background」** Sparse autoencoders are used in mechanistic interpretability to decompose neural network activations into interpretable features, but standard SAEs embed dictionary atoms in Euclidean space where volume grows polynomially. The author argues that concepts learned by LLMs form branching hierarchies whose volume grows exponentially, causing feature collisions at the boundary, dead latents, and reconstruction degradation at large dictionary sizes. Hyperbolic geometry, such as the Poincaré ball, provides exponentially growing volume and is therefore a candidate geometry for better matching hierarchical concept structure.

**「Impact」** Researchers and practitioners using sparse autoencoders for interpretability now have an open-source PyTorch implementation that claims better reconstruction, higher CE loss recovery, and drastically fewer dead latents without added inference cost; however, these improvements should be treated cautiously until independently replicated.
