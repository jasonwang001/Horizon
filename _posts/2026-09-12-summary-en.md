The affected range includes versions 18.7 through before 19.1.8, 19.2 versions before 19.2.6, and 19.3 versions before 19.3.2. GitLab strongly recommends that self-managed instances upgrade immediately, while GitLab.com has already been fixed and GitLab Dedicated users need no action. The flaw was reported by researcher s3ntago through HackerOne; GitLab has not publicly disclosed the specific preconditions, no reproducible public proof-of-concept exists, and there is no evidence of exploitation in the wild.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 11, 11:05

The CVSS 10.0 score is the maximum on the 0–10 severity scale, reserved for remotely exploitable bugs requiring no privileges or user interaction that compromise confidentiality, integrity and availability.

**「Impact」** Self-managed GitLab instances in the affected ranges \(18.7 through before 19.1.8, 19.2 before 19.2.6, and 19.3 before 19.3.2\) are exposed to unauthenticated reads of arbitrary files on the server, making immediate upgrade to the fixed releases the concrete required action for those operators. ### [210M text-to-image DiT trained from scratch on one GPU](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1wdfmvq/training_a_210m_texttoimage_dit_from_scratch_on/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A Reddit user reports training a 210M-parameter text-to-image diffusion transformer from scratch on a single RTX PRO 6000 over 3.5 days using 4.2M images at 256², and highlights three measurements from the run. The model uses a cross-attention DiT with 896 width and 16 blocks, 2D RoPE, QK-norm, SwiGLU, adaLN-single, rectified flow with logit-normal timesteps, and a timestep shift of 2.8 derived from the SD3/RAE rule for the 32-channel FLUX.2 latent. The author reports that 16 register tokens in the image stream and two learned key/value slots appended to every cross-attention shape attention: at mid-noise in a middle block, the two slots receive ~90% of cross-attention mass while the EOS token drops to ~4%, and register vectors grow to 4–13× the norm of image tokens by the middle blocks. The post also reports that flow-matching loss moved 0.805 → 0.754 while held-out FID improved 33.7 → 27.0, FD-DINOv2 570 → 218, and detector-based object accuracy 65% → 90%, arguing the loss is a training-health signal rather than a sample-quality signal. It further states the training-time timestep shift is worth more than doubling steps: on 2,456 held-out prompts, 20 steps with shift 2.8 gave FID 27.0, 50 steps gave 26.6, 8 steps gave 28.4, and 20 steps with no shift gave 27.3 with FD-DINOv2 228; code, write-up, weights, and a demo are released, but the results are self-reported and not peer-reviewed.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/IvanMikhnenkov · Sep 11, 13:00

**「Background」** Diffusion transformers \(DiTs\) apply the transformer architecture to image generation, and prior work such as Vision Transformers Need Registers found that attention sinks — tokens that absorb a disproportionate share of attention mass — can emerge on low-information inputs and distort attention maps, motivating explicit register tokens \[tool-1-1\]\[tool-1-2\]. Flow-matching or rectified-flow training with logit-normal timesteps and a resolution-dependent timestep shift was popularized by SD3 to balance sampling across different resolutions \[tool-2-2\]\[tool-2-3\]. Sample quality in these models is usually tracked with held-out metrics such as FID and FD-DINOv2 rather than the training objective itself, since loss values can move independently of perceptual quality.

**「Impact」** Developers exploring small-scale text-to-image DiTs can use the released tinyDiT code, weights, and demo as a concrete one-GPU baseline, but should treat the reported attention-sink and loss-quality findings as self-reported, non-peer-reviewed observations rather than settled results.
